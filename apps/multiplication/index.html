                How to play

                • On your turn, roll two dice. Draw a rectangle with those side lengths — a 3 and a 6 makes a 3×6 (or 6×3) rectangle.
                • Rectangles can't overlap anything already on the board.
                • Player 1's first rectangle must go in the top-left corner.
                • If your rectangle doesn't fit anywhere, you pass. When every player passes in a row, the game is over.
                • After you draw your rectangle, work out how many squares it has (like 3 × 4 = 12). Get it right to score those points!
                • You score one point per square. Most squares wins!
                • Watch the CPU — it shows how it counts its squares step by step.
                • Practice mode drills the times-table facts you find hardest, based on how you've answered before.

                Keys: Space roll · R rotate (click-to-place mode) · type your answer + Enter (Esc clears it)

                Drawing: press on a square and drag to the opposite corner. You can change this in Settings.

                Settings

                Theme

                "Auto" follows your device's light or dark mode.

                First rectangle

                "Top-left corner" is the original rule: it stops Player 1 from grabbing the best spot.

                Dice

                "Real dice": roll your own dice at the table and tap the numbers you got. CPU players still roll their own dice in the app.

                Rolling

                "Auto roll": your dice roll by themselves at the start of each turn. (With real dice you still roll at the table.)

                Only roll what fits

                "No room" means your roll doesn't fit anywhere, so you pass. Near the end: when the board is nearly full, only rolls that fit (adjusts to board size and difficulty). Always: nobody ever passes. Never: the original rules.

                Placing rectangles

                "Draw it": press and drag to draw a rectangle of the right size. "Click to place": the rectangle is made for you, you pick the spot. "Auto": the game places it for you, so you only do the math.

                CPU explanations

                How long each step of a CPU turn takes — every message, every square it draws and every number it counts. Slow: 2 seconds · Normal: 1 second · Fast: ½ second.

                CPU turns

                "Instant": the CPU plays its turn straight away without explaining, and it's your go again.

                After a CPU turn

                "Press a button" keeps the CPU's explanation on screen until you press the button to go on.

                Answer timer (Home multiplayer)

                Time to answer the multiplication in Home multiplayer games. Run out and you lose your turn: the rectangle comes off the board and scores nothing. The clock pauses during "Help me count". (Single player has its own timers under Expert.)

                Times-table grid (in Stats)

                Each cell can show its icon or your best time for that fact. Cells with no right answer yet keep the icon.
