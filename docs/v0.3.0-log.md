## Stage 0: Legend fix

**What changed** (`js/core.js`, `createPairDice` and `rollForBoard`):
- **The mix:** Legend deals a teen × 2–9 for half of its rolls and Tricky facts for the rest.
- **The size bias:** all pair dice (Tricky, Master, Legend) make big rectangles rarer on smaller boards, like the loaded dice on Easy, Medium and Hard do.
- **The fallback:** near the end, when none of the difficulty's facts fit the gaps left, the pair dice deal a smaller fact (up to 12 × 12) that does fit, so the board gets finished instead of both players passing.
- **Board size:** Legend now needs a 12×12 board or bigger (invites too).

Measured, rectangles per CPU-vs-CPU game (a unit test guards the minimums):

| Board | Hard | Tricky before → after | Legend before → after | Teen facts per Legend game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12×12 | 11.7 | 4.1 → 10.0 | 2.7 → 8.7 | 1.8 |
| 16×16 | 12.8 | 6.5 → 13.6 | 3.9 → 12.7 | 2.7 |
| 20×20 | 15.1 | 9.9 → 18.8 | 5.5 → 16.9 | 3.8 |
| 24×24 | 19.1 | 13.3 → 23.2 | 7.7 → 21.5 | 5.1 |

**Decisions made on your behalf:**
- **Teen share is 1/2, not the 1/3 in the plan.** With 1/3, the size bias and the end-of-game fill-in left only about 1 teen fact in a 12×12 game; 1/2 gives about 2 without making games shorter. It's `LEGEND_TEEN_SHARE` in core.js.
- **Tricky had the same problem** (4 rectangles on 12×12), so the size bias and fill-in apply to every pair-dice difficulty, not just Legend.
- **The fill-in facts can be easier than the difficulty** (for example 2 × 3 on Tricky). The alternative was ending games with a third of the board empty.

**How to try it:** single player → Legend (or type IDDQD after pressing ~ to unlock everything) → 12×12 or bigger → play. The hint under the board size explains the mix.

## Stage 1: accounts

**What's new:**
- **Server:**
  - `server/store.js` keeps everything in sqlite (`node:sqlite`, no npm packages), in `data/blockout.db` (`BLOCKOUT_DB` changes the path).
  - `server/auth.js` has the swappable sign-in providers:
    - dev: HMAC-signed tokens
    - Keycloak: RS256 tokens checked against the realm's published keys; the browser uses PKCE
  - `server/accounts.js` has the rules. The HTTP routes are in `server/classroom.js`.
- **Shared logic** (`js/progress.js`): an activity log, merging a device's save into an account, fact summaries and homework goals.
- **Browser:**
  - `js/auth.js`: sign-in, the token, and progress sync.
  - `js/accounts.js`: the new screens.
    - The account chip at the top of the menu.
    - A sign-in dialog, with play as a guest always offered.
    - The student's 📚 Homework and account page (classes, join by code, the code for a grown-up).
    - The teacher's 🏫 My classes (roster, stats, homework, tournaments).
    - The parent's 👪 My kids (link a child, their stats and homework, set extra homework).
  - A banner on the menu when a tournament is open.
- **Guests play exactly as before.** The only changes: hosting a class now needs a teacher sign-in, and there's a small "👤 Sign in" chip.
- **Privacy:** see [privacy.md](privacy.md).

**How to try it** (`node server/classroom.js 8080`, open http://localhost:8080):
1. **Teacher:**
   - Tap 👤 Sign in, choose Teacher, type a name, and pick Cadence Park.
   - Open 🏫 My classes and make a class: you get a 6-letter code and a join link.
   - Add class homework, and plan a tournament a few minutes ahead.
2. **Student** (another browser or a private window):
   - Open the join link, `…/#roster=CODE`, and sign in as a Student. The email is filled in as `name@iusd.org`. You join the class.
   - 📚 Homework shows what's set. Play; it ticks itself off.
   - Tap your name at the top for your classes and the code for a grown-up.
3. **Parent** (a third browser): sign in as a Parent, open 👪 My kids, type the child's code, open the child, and add homework.
4. **The tournament:** when it's due, the lobby opens by itself (the server checks every 10 s).
   - The teacher gets "Open the lobby" in My classes.
   - Students of that class, or of the whole school for a school tournament, see "🏆 … is open! Join" on the menu.
5. **Tests:** `npm test` (accounts, the HTTP API end to end, and the Keycloak token checks with a generated key).
6. **Keycloak later:** `BLOCKOUT_AUTH=keycloak KEYCLOAK_ISSUER=https://…/realms/NAME KEYCLOAK_CLIENT_ID=blockout node server/classroom.js`.

**Decisions made on your behalf:**
- **Hosting:** hosting any live class now needs a teacher sign-in, and the server enforces it. Students joining a live game still need no account.
- **Signed-in students in live games** play under their account's first name. No name check is needed, and nothing goes to OpenAI.
- **Roles from Keycloak:** a token's realm role `student`, `teacher` or `parent` decides the role. With no role, a school-domain account is a student and anyone else a parent.
- **Dev sign-in:**
  - Teachers pick a school; that gives them a made-up `teacher@iusd.org`, used only for its domain.
  - The same name, role and email always reach the same account.
  - The token secret is kept in the database, so sign-ins survive restarts.
- **Separate saves:** a signed-in player's save is kept under its own key in the browser, so the guest save on a shared Chromebook isn't touched.
- **First use on a device:** the device's guest save is merged into the account once: the most points, every unlock and achievement, and for each fact the record that has seen it most. After that the server copy wins, and the device keeps a cache.
- **Cheats stay on the device.** The server stores saves with cheats and what they unlocked removed, and IDDQD is switched back on locally after syncing.
- **"Master the ×N table" homework** means every N × 1…10 and 1…10 × N fact is Mastered (19 facts).
- **Homework goals:** master a table; finish N practice rounds (any difficulty or one); beat the CPU on a board this big or bigger; finish N games. Rounds, wins and games count from when the homework was set.
- **Due dates:** late means past the due date's end, by the server's day.
- **Class and parent codes:** class codes are 6 characters and parent codes 8, with no 0/O/1/I/L so they're easy to read out.
- **Codes don't expire yet.** Parent codes stay the same until they're reset (not built yet).
- **Scheduled tournaments** use Easy, Medium or Hard and boards up to 12×12, like the lobby. The teacher needs to open the lobby from My classes to run it, just as with any tournament.
- **Keycloak token refresh isn't built.** When the access token expires, the player is signed out and signs in again.

**Later changes you asked for** (made during Stage 2, in the new layout):
- **Homework only offers what the child can already play:**
  - The kinds of homework, times tables, difficulties and board sizes come from the child's unlocks (`Progress.availableOptions`). Class homework uses what every student in the class has (`sharedOptions`); a class with nobody in it gets a new player's options.
  - "Master a table" and "practice rounds" appear once practice (and a table) is unlocked.
- **"Play ›" on each open homework** sets up the menu and starts the game:
  - "Master ×7": practice on a difficulty that has ×7, with only ×7 picked.
  - Practice rounds: practice on that difficulty.
  - Beat the CPU: single player on that board and difficulty.
  - Games: single player.

**Open questions:**
- Deleting a student's data from the teacher's screen (only a manual database task for now).
- Should parent codes expire, or be resettable?
- Keycloak isn't tested against a real server yet (only with a generated key in the unit tests).

## Stage 2: Turborepo monorepo

**The layout** (details in README.md and CLAUDE.md):

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `apps/multiplication` | The main game, at `/` |
| `apps/{addition,subtraction,division,fraction-add,fraction-subtract,fraction-multiply,fraction-divide}` | One app per game |
| `apps/more-games` | The hub |
| `apps/server` | The server, now ES modules |
| `packages/engine` | Board, dice, CPU, and the other operations' rules (`@blockout/engine/{fraction,boards,variants}`) |
| `packages/progress` | Progress, plus the Wardrobe |
| `packages/ui` | CSS, icons and the page plugin |
| `packages/game-kit` | The other games' engine |
| `packages/classroom`, `packages/auth` | The browser clients |

- **Tooling:** npm workspaces, Turborepo, Vite 8 and ESLint 10. Root scripts are `dev`, `build`, `test`, `lint` and `start` (build, then serve on 8080).
- **How the 6,300-line `game.js` was split,** keeping behaviour identical:
  - A script with a proper JavaScript parser and scope analysis (espree + eslint-scope, run once, not kept) cut it into 15 modules along its own section headers.
  - Each module only declares things at import. What used to run at load moved into its `run()`, and `game/index.js` calls them in the original order.
  - Variables written from other modules got setters (only 4 needed: `game`, `gameId`, `practice`, `wardrobeFilter`).
- **No more globals:** `window.BlockoutX` became imports, the account screens import from `game/api.js`, and the vendored QR library became the `qrcode-generator` npm package.
- **Styles:** the stylesheet was split along its sections into `@blockout/ui` (shared) and the app (classroom and account screens). They're imported in the original order, so the cascade is unchanged.
- **Icons and theme:** the icon sprite (the main game's plus two from the other games) and the theme script are added to every page by `@blockout/ui/vite`.
- **Tests:** they moved next to their code: 123 in all, the 122 from before plus one for homework options.

**Checks** (the same Playwright pass before and after the move, on the same flows):
- Single player, practice, Home multiplayer, shop + Wardrobe + cheats, whole class, pairs and a tournament all PASS, with no page errors, before and after.
- The accounts flow PASSES on the new build: sign-in, class roster, homework (limited options, Play ›), parent link, the scheduled tournament opening, and phone width.
- The other games: single player and practice play through on the new URLs.

**Decisions made on your behalf:**
- **Seven small apps:** each game is its own app, and all of them run the shared `@blockout/game-kit`.
- **URLs:** the More games hub is its own small app at `/more-games/`, and the games are at `/<name>/`. The old `prototypes/…html` addresses are gone; the More games button points at `/more-games/`.
- **CSS ownership:** most of the CSS is shared by all games, so it's in `@blockout/ui`. Only the classroom and account screens' CSS stays in the multiplication app.
- **JavaScript** stays plain JS (no TypeScript), as the plan says, and the code keeps its style.
- **Dev mode:** `npm run dev` runs a Vite dev server per app and the API server; the multiplication app proxies `/api` to it. For the full site with every game at its real address, use `npm start`.
- **Data:** the sqlite database stays at `data/blockout.db` in the repo root.
- **The debug hook:** `window.ProtoGame` now also has `boards`, for automated checks of the other games.

**How to try it:** `npm install`, then `npm start`, then open http://localhost:8080. The More games button leads to the other games. Run `npm test` and `npm run lint` for the checks.

## Stage 3: the other games

**What's new in each of the seven games** (all in `@blockout/game-kit`):
- **Home multiplayer:** two players on one screen, taking turns (it's an unlock).
- **Points** in the game's own wallet:
  - A right answer earns 1, +2 for right first time and +5 on every 5th in a row. Expert practice doubles it.
  - Winning earns +10/+20/+40 on Easy/Medium/Hard.
  - Finishing a practice round earns +10 for 10 questions and +30 for 20, doubled on Expert.
- **Shop** with skill-tree chains: Medium (120), then Hard (250); 20-question practice (60), then Expert practice (150); Home multiplayer (150). The next step shows its price, later ones are blurred placeholders, and tapping a locked choice on the menu offers to unlock it.
- **Achievements** (11, plus "Blockout!" in the fraction-bar games): first game, winner, Medium and Hard champion, ten wins, 5 and 10 in a row, 100 right, first, perfect and Expert practice. They use the same WoW-style banners, toasts and confetti as the main game (now shared as `@blockout/ui/rewards`).
- **Stats:** totals (games, wins, practice rounds, best streak, points) and every question of every practice family, coloured Mastered / Learning / Needs practice / Not seen yet by the same rules as the Times Table.
- **Each game's own maths explanations** are unchanged ("Show me how", the CPU's working).

**Checks:**
- **Unit tests:** `packages/game-kit/test/meta.test.js` covers the shop chains, points, achievements and saves; 128 tests in all.
- **Playwright:**
  - All seven games play a full game against the CPU; Learn practice rounds finish; no page errors.
  - Addition as a new player: the locks are right; a full game earned points and four achievements.
  - A player with points bought Medium in the shop; Home multiplayer starts with "Player 1's turn".
  - Screenshots are in the docs (Playing → More games).

**Decisions made on your behalf:**
- **Separate saves:** each game has its own save and wallet (`blockout.game.<id>`), separate from the multiplication game's. Sharing one wallet across games would mix unrelated unlocks; it can be joined later.
- **Now unlocked with points:** Medium, Hard, 20-question practice and Expert practice were free in the prototypes. Only Easy, 10-question practice and all practice families stay free.
- **No points at home:** Home multiplayer games earn no points or achievements, like the main game's Home games ("just for fun").
- **Not added to the other games** (the plan said "where it makes sense"):
  - **Classroom play and invites:** the classroom server's rooms deal multiplication rolls. Supporting other operations means making rooms operation-aware, which is a real project.
  - **Accounts and homework:** those follow the multiplication game's save.
  - **Stickers and the Wardrobe.**
  - **Timer lengths:** Expert practice keeps each game's fixed timer rather than 30/10/5-second unlocks.

**Follow-up:** the multiplication game still has its own copy of the toasts, banners and confetti (with the Wardrobe's emoji confetti). Moving it onto `@blockout/ui/rewards` is a small next step.
